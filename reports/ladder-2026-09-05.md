# The similarity ladder: no threshold on word overlap can catch this — 2026-09-05

**Five manifests, one field different between them, and the answer to item 22's option (a) is no** — but not for the reason it was predicted, and the arm that broke the pattern broke the design with it. That is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

The question: the linter grades two tools sharing a description by **word overlap** (`similarity()`, threshold 0.70). If that number separates the harmful case from the harmless one at *some* value, lowering the threshold is worth its false positives. If it does not, no threshold works and option (a) is dead.

## The ladder

Every rung is `ablate-pair` with exactly one field changed — the competitor's description — enforced field by field by a test. `sum_by_category` against the clean arm's **95.0%** [86.3, 98.3]:

| Competitor's description | Sim. to sibling | Sim. to the requests | Linter | Rate | → competitor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| byte-identical | **1.000** | 0.080 | `duplicate` (error) | **48.3%** [36.2, 60.7] | 24 of 31 |
| one word changed | **0.857** | 0.080 | `near-duplicate` (warning) | **56.7%** [44.1, 68.4] | 20 of 26 |
| a paraphrase | **0.529** | 0.080 | **silent** | **50.0%** [37.7, 62.3] | 25 of 30 |
| different wording throughout | **0.130** | 0.048 | **silent** | **58.3%** [45.7, 69.9] | 20 of 25 |
| effectively disjoint vocabulary | **0.043** | 0.026 | **silent** | **83.3%** [72.0, 90.7] | 5 of 10 |

All five at 60/60 trials, 3 sessions × 1 repeat, gates pass. Runs: [`…sim086`](twin-ablate-pair-sim086-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md), [`…sim013`](twin-ablate-pair-sim013-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md), [`…sim004`](twin-ablate-pair-sim004-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md), plus [`ablate-pair`](twin-ablate-pair-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md) and [`…paraphrased`](twin-ablate-pair-paraphrased-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md). Fixture `1.5.0`; three of the five needed a `--resume`, one of them twice.

## What it says

**Over the top four rungs the rate is flat.** 48.3, 56.7, 50.0, 58.3 — every interval overlaps every other. Word overlap with the sibling falls from **1.000 to 0.130**, a range covering byte-identical, near-identical, paraphrased and wholly rewritten, and the harm does not move. **No threshold placed anywhere in that range separates a harmful competitor from a harmless one**, so option (a) — lower `nearDuplicateThreshold` — cannot work on the evidence. That verdict was the point of the ladder and it holds.

**The fifth rung recovered, and it also broke the design.** At 0.043 the rate jumps to 83.3% and the competitor stops absorbing failures — 5 instead of 20 to 25. But that rung's description is not only distant from its sibling, it is distant from *the requests*: mean similarity to the 20 utterances falls from **0.080** on the top three rungs to **0.026**. The two variables moved together, exactly as this arm's registration warned they might: *"the ladder cannot hold similarity-to-the-utterance perfectly fixed while it varies similarity-to-the-sibling."*

So the recovery has two readings and this design cannot choose between them:

1. Lexical distance from the sibling starts to matter, but only somewhere below 0.13 — a threshold so low it would fire on almost any pair of vague descriptions.
2. The competitor simply stopped looking relevant to the request, so it stopped competing. That is a property of description-versus-*utterance*, which the linter has no access to at all: it never sees what users will ask.

Reading 2 is the better fit, because the rung at 0.130 — already almost disjoint from its sibling — is still fully collapsed at 58.3%, and its utterance-similarity (0.048) sits between the flat band's 0.080 and the recovered rung's 0.026. The harm tracks **relevance to the request**, not resemblance to the neighbour. Either way, **option (a) is not rescued**: on reading 1 the useful threshold is below anything shippable, and on reading 2 the linter is measuring the wrong quantity entirely.

## The predictions, scored — one was wrong

Registered in `2bc803b` before any rung ran.

| Rung | Predicted | Measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.857 | 45–60% | 56.7% | ✅ |
| 0.130 | 45–60% | 58.3% | ✅ |
| 0.043 | 45–60%, "falsified at 85% or above" | **83.3%** | ❌ **band wrong**, falsifier survived by 1.7 points |

The third prediction is a miss and is scored as one. It cleared its own stated falsification line by less than two points, which is not a vindication — the band was 45–60 and the arm read 83.3. What survives is the *conclusion* it was defending, and it survives for a reason the prediction did not name: the confound, not the mechanism. A prediction that is right about the verdict and wrong about the number is a prediction that got lucky, and the honest record says so.

The reasoning that failed is identifiable: it treated "uninformative" as a property the rung's text either has or lacks, when informativeness is relative to *the request*, and rewriting a description into unrelated vocabulary makes it less relevant to the request as a side effect. Holding that constant while varying sibling overlap would need utterance-anchored rewrites and is the measurement this one could not be.

## What this changes

- **Item 22's option (a) is closed on evidence**, not on taste. Lowering the similarity threshold cannot separate the cases over the range where the confound is controlled.
- **Option (b) — a structural proxy on close *names* plus uninformative descriptions — gains support**, because names are where the decision landed in every collapsed rung: the competitor took 20 to 25 of every rung's failures while its description said nothing useful. It still has no measurement behind its false-positive rate.
- **Option (c) remains the default**: state the ceiling, keep `lint` model-free.
- **New open question, sharper than the one it replaces:** the harm tracks description-versus-request relevance, which no static linter can see because it does not know the requests. That is not a gap in this linter's rules; it is an argument that L0 cannot in principle catch this class, and L1 — which does see requests — is the only place it can be caught.

## Bounds

One tool pair, one page, one judge, three sessions, n = 20 per session per rung. Similarity is Jaccard over lowercased word tokens, the linter's own measure, recomputed from the text by a test rather than declared. Utterance-similarity uses the same measure and is a crude proxy for "relevance", not a semantic judgement. Five rungs is a shape, not a curve.
