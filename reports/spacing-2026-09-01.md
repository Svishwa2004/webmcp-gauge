# Does spacing the sessions change the answer? 480 trials across 26 hours

**Answer: the σ does not care — between-session spread with sessions 9–17 h apart is indistinguishable from back-to-back — but both mid-range tools declined monotonically across the 26-hour span, which is the shape slow drift looks like.** Measured 2026-08-31 → 2026-09-01.

Every number this project has published came from sessions minutes apart. [`twin-degraded-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md`](twin-degraded-1.3.0-glm-5.3-s3r1.md) said so in its own caveats: back-to-back sessions measure process and browser independence, not day-to-day drift. This run is that arm re-measured with the sessions spread across a day and a night — same page, same frozen `1.3.0` utterances, same judge, same machine, same shape. The clock is the only difference.

## Design

Degraded twin, 3 sessions × 1 repeat over all 160 utterances, concurrency 3, judge `glm-5.3` — byte-for-byte the shape of the back-to-back arm, which finished at 03:30 local on 2026-08-31. The spaced sessions then ran:

| Session | Started (local) | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-08-31 04:18 | — (48 min after the back-to-back arm completed) |
| 2 | 2026-08-31 21:29 | **17.2 h** |
| 3 | 2026-09-01 06:41 | **9.2 h** |

**480 of 480 trials measured.** 31 non-measurements at their sessions (2 `judge_truncated`, 29 `judge_unavailable`) were retried by `--resume` at the reconcile (06:53–07:09), so 31 trials — 6.5% — carry the reconcile's clock rather than their session's: 2/160 in session 1, 17/160 in session 2, 12/160 in session 3.

The schedule that was supposed to produce 6 h gaps was refused twice by the Windows Task Scheduler (battery defaults — the operational record is in `PROJECT-LOG.md` and [`scripts/README.md`](../scripts/README.md)), and session 3 was run by hand. The arm measures **"sessions between nine and seventeen hours apart, spanning a day boundary"**, not six hours apart; the gaps are unequal, so no single spacing owns the σ below.

### σ did not grow with spacing

Worst-case between-session σ: **0.085 spaced against 0.062 back-to-back** — and the two load-bearing tools swap places (`sum_by_category` is worse spaced, `top_expenses` worse back-to-back). Both sit far below the smallest effect this project claims, 0.35. A reproducibility figure measured on back-to-back sessions does not mislead when the sessions are hours apart.

### But the per-session shape is what drift looks like

| Tool | Arm | s1 | s2 | s3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sum_by_category` | back-to-back (minutes apart) | 60 | 55 | 65 |
| | spaced (17.2 h / 9.2 h) | 60 | 45 | **40** |
| `top_expenses` | back-to-back | 35 | 20 | 25 |
| | spaced | 40 | 35 | **30** |

The back-to-back sessions wander non-monotonically around their mean — that is noise. Both of the spaced arm's mid-range tools decline **monotonically** across 26 hours, from a starting point that agrees with the back-to-back arm measured 48 minutes earlier (60 on `sum_by_category` in both).

Three honest limits on that reading:

- n is 20 per session per tool. A three-session monotone ordering has a 1-in-6 chance under a stable mean; both mid-range tools showing it is 1-in-36 — and the two orderings share a judge and a clock, so treat that arithmetic as a description, not a p-value.
- The refill contamination does not explain it. Session 2's rate computed from its at-21:29 trials only is 44.4% on `sum_by_category` (8/18) and 37.5% on `top_expenses` (6/16), against 45% and 35% with the late refills included — the decline is the same with or without the reconcile's clock.
- The definitive design — one arm back-to-back and one arm spread over the *same* 26 hours — was not run. The back-to-back arm here finished 48 minutes before the spaced arm began, which is close, but a same-window interleaving is the control that would separate drift from noise. Recorded as the follow-up if the drift reading matters to anyone.

### The judge has a per-prompt ceiling problem

`find_anomalies-10` timed out five times across 20 minutes (four `judge_unavailable`, then success on the fifth) while the 30 trials refilled around it all succeeded. The same prompt measured in 15.9 s (session 1) and then **48.8 s / 2,755 completion tokens** (session 2) against the judge's 60 s timeout — reasoning on this prompt rides near the ceiling, and the provider's conditions decide which side of it lands. Two consequences:

- For CI use, the `--resume` loop is not optional. One sticky trial held the whole 480-trial arm at exit 2 for four consecutive passes.
- The timeout was **not** raised mid-arm to fetch the trial: the boundary between "measurement" and `judge_unavailable` is part of the instrument, and moving it between sessions of one arm is worse than an incomplete arm. The fifth attempt succeeded with the instrument unchanged.

### Provider reliability by hour: three points, no claim

Non-measurements at first attempt, per session: **2/160 at 04:18, 17/160 at 21:29, 12/160 at 06:41.** An order of magnitude apart, but three sessions are three points, not evidence — recorded so the next arms keep counting.

## What this changes

- The back-to-back σ figures (0.041 / 0.062) stand as reproducibility numbers. Spacing sessions to 9–17 h does not invalidate them.
- **Cross-day comparisons now carry a caveat that back-to-back runs could not see.** Point estimates of mid-range rates moved 8–12 points between 2026-08-30 and 09-01 (all intervals overlapping): `sum_by_category` 60.0 → 48.3, `top_expenses` 26.7 → 35.0. A single arm compared with itself across days inherits the drift question; a single arm measured at one time remains sound.
- PROJECT-LOG item 6 is closed. The spacing axis is measured at 9–17 h, day-spanning. The planned 6 h design never ran and is not being re-run — the scheduler's refused firings bought a wider manipulation than the one designed.
